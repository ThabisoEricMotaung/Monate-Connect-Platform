# How to Write an RFQ That Attracts Quality Responses

**Category:** Buyer Guides

**Author:** AiForm Procurement Team  
**Reading Time:** 8 minutes

## Introduction

An RFQ (Request for Quotation) is one of the most important documents a buyer creates. Done well, it attracts quality suppliers and generates competitive, compliant quotes. Done poorly, it leads to late responses, incomplete information, and suppliers who don't meet your standards.

This guide walks through the anatomy of an effective RFQ—the structure, language, requirements, and evaluation criteria that actually work. We'll show you how professional procurement teams write RFQs that move fast and attract the right suppliers.

---

## Why Your RFQ Design Matters

Your RFQ is more than a shopping list. It's your first conversation with potential suppliers. It sets expectations about:
- What you need (scope and specifications)
- How you evaluate (your criteria)
- Who you'll work with (your standards)
- How you operate (timeline and processes)

A well-written RFQ attracts better suppliers. A confusing one wastes time for everyone.

---

## Section 1: The Header and Context

### Start with clarity

Your RFQ header should answer these questions immediately:

**What is this?**
- RFQ number and date
- Department or buyer name
- Brief one-line description of what you're requesting

**When do you need it?**
- Closing date and time (be specific: "17:00 SAST, 28 August 2026")
- Expected decision date
- Start date for the contract or delivery

**Who's asking?**
- Contact person name, email, phone
- Submission instructions (email address, portal, physical address)
- Questions email or process

Example header:
```
REQUEST FOR QUOTATION (RFQ)

RFQ Number: 2026-08-001
Closing Date: 28 August 2026 at 17:00 SAST
Contact: procurement@yourbusiness.co.za
Telephone: +27 (0)11 XXX XXXX

SCOPE: Supply and delivery of office furniture (desks, chairs, storage)
Expected delivery: October 2026
```

### Include selection criteria upfront

Before suppliers spend time writing a response, they need to know how you'll evaluate them. List your evaluation criteria in order of importance:

1. **Price (40%)** – Best value for quality specified
2. **Delivery timeline (25%)** – Ability to deliver by October 2026
3. **B-BBEE status (20%)** – Minimum level 4
4. **Quality/warranty (10%)** – Warranty terms and quality assurance
5. **References (5%)** – Track record with similar orders

This prevents suppliers from optimising the wrong things.

---

## Section 2: Detailed Scope and Specifications

Vague scopes attract vague responses. Be specific.

### For products, specify:
- Brand/model or equivalent (with clear approval process for alternatives)
- Quantity and unit of measure
- Colours, sizes, finishes
- Packaging requirements
- Delivery location and method
- Quality standards (ISO certifications, test results, inspection processes)

### For services, specify:
- Scope of work (what's included, what's excluded)
- Deliverables (reports, templates, training materials, etc.)
- Timeline with milestones
- Resource requirements (team size, qualifications, experience)
- Support and maintenance terms
- Intellectual property rights

**Bad:** "Supply office furniture for our head office"

**Better:**
```
Supply and deliver 20 x office desks (minimum 1.4m x 0.7m working surface), 
20 x ergonomic office chairs (black, with lumbar support, 12-month warranty), 
and 8 x 4-drawer filing cabinets (grey, locking drawers).

All items must meet South African safety standards. 
Delivery to head office, Floor 3, by 30 September 2026.
Full warranty and support for 12 months included.
```

---

## Section 3: Commercial Terms

Suppliers need to know your non-negotiables before they bid.

### Payment terms
- Net 30, 60, or 90 days from invoice?
- Deposit required? If so, how much and when?
- Payment method (bank transfer, cheque, credit card)?

### Compliance and insurance
- Public liability insurance amount required?
- Professional indemnity (for services)?
- B-BBEE verification required? What level?
- Tax clearance certificate required?

### Warranties and guarantees
- Product warranty duration and terms?
- Service warranty or SLA?
- Replacement/remediation process for defects?

### Liability and intellectual property
- Who owns deliverables?
- Limitation of liability caps?
- Confidentiality requirements?

## Section 4: The Quote Format

Make it easy for suppliers to quote consistently. Provide a template with line items:

| Item | Description | Quantity | Unit Price | Total | Notes |
|------|-------------|----------|-----------|-------|-------|
| 1 | Office desk (as per spec) | 20 | R _____ | R _____ | |
| 2 | Office chair (as per spec) | 20 | R _____ | R _____ | |
| 3 | Filing cabinet (as per spec) | 8 | R _____ | R _____ | |
| | **Delivery** | | | R _____ | Included in above? |
| | **Installation** | | | R _____ | If applicable |
| | **Subtotal** | | | **R _____** | |
| | **VAT (15%)** | | | **R _____** | |
| | **TOTAL** | | | **R _____** | |

Ask suppliers to also provide:
- Breakdown of unit costs vs. delivery
- Lead time from order confirmation
- Any volume discounts if order changes
- Key warranty and support terms
- B-BBEE level certificate and date of validity
- References (name, contact, dates of similar work)

---

## Section 5: Quality Assurance and References

Asking for references shows you take quality seriously.

### Reference questions:
- "Provide 2–3 references for similar projects completed in the last 2 years"
- "Reference contact: [name, phone, email]"
- "Scope of similar work and dates"

### Quality assurance:
- "Describe your quality control process"
- "What certifications do you hold? (ISO 9001, etc.)"
- "How do you handle defects or failed deliverables?"
- "What's your warranty and remediation process?"

---

## Section 6: Timeline and Next Steps

Be crystal clear about the process:

**RFQ Timeline:**
- **Closing date:** 28 August 2026, 17:00 SAST
- **Evaluation period:** 29 August – 3 September 2026
- **Supplier notification:** 4 September 2026
- **Contract signature:** 7 September 2026
- **Delivery start:** 15 September 2026

**Supplier responsibilities:**
1. Submit complete quote by closing date
2. Be available for follow-up questions (if selected)
3. Provide certificates and references
4. Sign contract within 5 days of notification

---

## Section 7: Common Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. Being too vague about what you want
Suppliers will guess, and they'll guess wrong. Specificity saves time.

### 2. Not stating evaluation criteria
Suppliers optimise for the wrong things (lowest price, not best value).

### 3. Asking for too much information
If you ask for 20 pages of response when a 3-page quote will do, you'll scare away good suppliers.

### 4. Not including compliance requirements upfront
If B-BBEE verification is essential, say so. Don't surprise the winner at the end.

### 5. Setting unrealistic timelines
A week to quote on a complex service isn't realistic. Give 2–3 weeks for major RFQs.

### 6. Not specifying what happens next
Will the lowest bidder always win? Will you negotiate? Do suppliers need to attend a site visit?

## Section 9: After Closing: Evaluation

Once quotes come in, evaluate fairly and consistently:

1. **Compliance check:** Do quotes meet all mandatory requirements? (B-BBEE level, payment terms, warranty?)
2. **Score by criteria:** Use your pre-announced criteria (price 40%, delivery 25%, etc.)
3. **Compare total cost of ownership, not just price**
   - Warranty, support, and maintenance
   - Delivery costs included or separate?
   - Reference quality and risk

4. **Shortlist and interview:** For significant orders, call top 2–3 suppliers to clarify anything unclear

5. **Document your decision** – Keep notes on why you selected the winner. You may need to defend it later.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Clarity attracts quality.** The better your RFQ, the better your responses.
- **State your criteria upfront.** Suppliers will optimise for what matters to you.
- **Use a template.** Consistency speeds evaluation and reduces errors.
- **Include compliance early.** B-BBEE, tax clearance, insurance—surface these upfront.
- **Give realistic timelines.** Good suppliers are busy; rushing leads to poor responses.
- **Document your decision.** Track what worked and what didn't for next time.
