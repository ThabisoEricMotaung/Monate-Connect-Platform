## Introduction

B-BBEE (Broad-Based Black Economic Empowerment) is non-negotiable in South African procurement. For buyers, it's a legal requirement for government contracts and a strategic priority for private sector procurement. For suppliers, B-BBEE status directly affects their competitiveness.

Yet many suppliers and even procurement professionals don't fully understand what B-BBEE verification means, how the scoring works, or what changes you need to be aware of. This guide clarifies B-BBEE for both sides and shows how to verify and evaluate it correctly.

---

## What is B-BBEE?

B-BBEE is a South African policy framework designed to transform the economy and create economic opportunities for previously disadvantaged individuals (PDIs) and groups. The framework uses an **enterprise** scorecard to measure black ownership, management, employment equity, and skills development across a business.

### Why it matters in procurement

- **Government contracts:** Public-sector procurement is legally required to support B-BBEE suppliers
- **Private sector:** Many private companies use B-BBEE as a procurement criterion
- **Tender evaluation:** B-BBEE scoresheets directly affect bid acceptance and contract award
- **Supplier development:** Companies invest in supplier B-BBEE improvement to strengthen their supply chain

---

## The B-BBEE Scorecard: Core Elements

B-BBEE is measured on an **enterprise scorecard** with 7 core pillars:

### 1. **Ownership (25 points)**
The most visible pillar. Measures black ownership percentage:
- **100%+ of black shareholding** = 25 points
- **75%–99%** = Between 20–24 points
- **50%–74%** = Between 10–19 points
- **1%–49%** = Between 1–9 points

*Note:* "Black" includes African, Coloured, and Indian South Africans as defined by the Employment Equity Act. Women ownership attracts bonus points.

### 2. **Management Control (10 points)**
Measures black representation in senior management (directors and C-suite):
- **80%+ black senior management** = 10 points
- **60%–79%** = 5–9 points
- **40%–59%** = 3–4 points
- Below 40% = Lower scores

Executive management roles matter more here. Having one black director on a 10-person board isn't enough.

### 3. **Employment Equity (10 points)**
Measures black representation across all employee levels:
- **80%+ black employees** = 10 points
- **60%–79%** = 5–9 points
- **40%–59%** = 3–4 points

This is often easier to score on than ownership and management—many service companies score well here.

### 4. **Skills Development (10 points)**
Measures investment in training and development of PDI staff:
- Percentage of payroll spent on training
- Number of apprentices or interns mentored
- Training programmes focused on skills gaps
- **2%+ of payroll on PDI training** = 10 points

### 5. **Enterprise & Supplier Development (15 points)**
Measures support for black-owned suppliers and enterprises:
- Percentage of procurement spend with B-BBEE suppliers
- Direct investment in black suppliers
- Mentoring programmes
- **80%+ procurement from B-BBEE suppliers** = Higher scores

Private companies particularly value this—it shows commitment to the ecosystem.

### 6. **Socio-Economic Development (15 points)**
Measures corporate social responsibility and community investment:
- Spend on community development projects
- Educational grants
- Health and safety initiatives
- Environmental projects
- **1%+ of payroll on SED** = Higher scores

### 7. **Black Women Entrepreneurship (5 points)**
Bonus points for companies owned or managed by black women. This is emerging as increasingly important.

## Calculating Your B-BBEE Level

Scores add up to **100 points total**. Based on your overall score, you're assigned a **B-BBEE level**:

| Level | Points | Recognition |
|-------|--------|-------------|
| **Level 1** | 95–100 | Highest recognition |
| **Level 2** | 85–94 | |
| **Level 3** | 75–84 | |
| **Level 4** | 65–74 | *Minimum for many government contracts* |
| **Level 5** | 55–64 | |
| **Level 6** | 45–54 | |
| **Level 7** | 35–44 | |
| **Level 8** | 25–34 | |
| **Non-compliant** | <25 | Cannot use B-BBEE status in procurement |

**What this means:**
- Most buyers require **Level 4 or better** for contract eligibility
- Government procurement often requires Level 3 or better
- Some government departments are stricter (Level 1–2)
- A company with **no formal B-BBEE rating** is "non-compliant" and ineligible for B-BBEE scoring

## B-BBEE Verification: How It Works

### Who verifies?
Companies are verified by **B-BBEE verification agencies** accredited by the **South African National Accreditation System (SANAS)**. These are independent third parties.

### What happens during verification?
1. **Document review** – Bank statements, shareholder registers, payroll, training records
2. **Interviews** – Discussion with management about ownership, strategy, and development
3. **Site visit** – Physical verification of operations and staff presence
4. **Report generation** – A formal scoresheet is issued

### The verification certificate
Once verified, a company receives a **B-BBEE Verification Certificate** that includes:
- Company name and registration number
- B-BBEE level achieved
- Date of verification
- Expiry date (usually 12 months)
- Breakdown of scores by pillar

## What Suppliers Need to Know

### 1. Get verified if you compete for tenders
If your business regularly competes in government procurement or pursues contracts that list B-BBEE as a criterion, **get verified**. Costs typically range from **R3,000–R8,000** and take 4–8 weeks.

### 2. Keep records
Verification agencies will ask for:
- **Ownership documents:** Shareholder registers, ID documents, bank accounts showing cash contributions
- **Management:** Organogram, CVs of senior management, minutes of board meetings
- **Employment records:** Payroll records, employment contracts, ID documents of staff
- **Training:** Training course invoices, attendance registers, skills development plans
- **SED projects:** Invoices, proof of payment, project documentation

*Pro tip:* Maintain a B-BBEE file with these documents updated quarterly.

### 3. Understand scoring is relative
Your B-BBEE level depends on your **company profile** (turnover, age, industry), not just raw numbers. A Level 6 startup may actually be performing better than a Level 5 mature business in its sector.

### 4. Renewal is annual
Your verification certificate expires annually. Renew 2–3 months before expiry to ensure no gaps during tender periods.

### 5. Improvement is strategic
Work with your verification agency or a B-BBEE consultant to understand where your company scores lowest. Often, the **cheapest improvements** are in skills development and supplier development—not ownership restructuring.

## What Buyers Need to Know

### 1. Verify the certificate is valid
When a supplier submits a B-BBEE certificate:
- Check the **expiry date** (is it current?)
- Confirm it's from a **SANAS-accredited agency** (not self-certified)
- Verify the **company details match** (company name, registration number)

Many unscrupulous suppliers use expired or fraudulent certificates. Spot checks help.

### 2. Understand Level 4 isn't "poor"
Government procurement often mandates Level 4 as the **minimum**. This doesn't mean Level 4 companies are weak—they're simply evaluated against the full 100-point scorecard. Many strong, growing businesses are Level 4 or 5.

### 3. Use B-BBEE as part of a broader evaluation
B-BBEE should be weighted **alongside price, quality, and delivery capacity**. Over-weighting B-BBEE (e.g., 50% of the evaluation) can lead to selecting less capable suppliers.

Balanced procurement: **B-BBEE 20% + Price 40% + Quality 25% + Delivery 15%**

### 4. Reserve supplier development for strategic partners
B-BBEE scorecards often include "supplier development" as a points pillar. If you have long-term relationships with B-BBEE suppliers, **mentoring and investment in their business** helps them improve—which strengthens your supply chain.

### 5. Verify trends year-on-year
If a key supplier's B-BBEE level drops significantly, ask why. This can signal internal business changes—turnover in leadership, cash flow challenges, or loss of black management.

## Recent Changes to B-BBEE (2024–2026)

### Amended Codes of Good Practice (2023 onwards)
The Department of Trade, Industry & Competition (DTIC) released updates:

1. **Increased focus on black women**
   - Bonus points for women in leadership and ownership
   - Gender disaggregation in employment equity data

2. **Skills Development tracking**
   - Greater emphasis on STEM and critical skills
   - Requirement to align training with sectoral needs

3. **Supply chain transparency**
   - More detailed supplier development reporting
   - Tracking of subcontractor B-BBEE status

4. **Simplified scorecards for small businesses**
   - Startups and Level 1 SMME suppliers have lower assessment burdens

*Always check the latest DTIC guidelines before structuring procurement or scoring.*

## Practical Tips: Verification Checklist

### For Suppliers getting verified:
- [ ] Compile shareholder register and proof of ownership (bank deposits)
- [ ] Prepare employment equity data (headcount by race and gender)
- [ ] Gather training invoices and attendance records for last 3 years
- [ ] Document SED projects: invoices, partners, beneficiaries
- [ ] Prepare organogram and management CV summaries
- [ ] Contact a SANAS-accredited verification agency
- [ ] Budget 4–8 weeks and R3,000–R8,000
- [ ] Renew certificate annually, 2–3 months before expiry

### For Buyers evaluating B-BBEE:
- [ ] Request **current** B-BBEE certificates (check expiry)
- [ ] Verify against the SANAS agency list
- [ ] Cross-check company details (name, registration number)
- [ ] Review trend (Is the supplier improving or declining?)
- [ ] Contextualize by sector (Is Level 4 strong or weak for that industry?)
- [ ] Weight B-BBEE appropriately in procurement criteria

## Common Misconceptions

### "Level 1 = best, Level 8 = worst"
**Not quite.** A startup with Level 5 is performing better than a mature company with Level 7. Levels are scored against company size and age.

### "Black ownership only"
**Ownership is 25 points, not 100.** Management, employment, skills, and supplier development add up to 75 more points. Many strong companies score well without majority black ownership.

### "One-time effort"
**B-BBEE requires annual renewal and continuous improvement.** Companies that ignore it will see scores drop.

### "Self-certification is enough"
**No.** Only SANAS-accredited verification agencies' certificates are valid in formal procurement.

## Key Takeaways

- **B-BBEE is structural:** It measures ownership, management, employment, skills, and supply chain development.
- **Level 4+ is standard:** Most buyers require this minimum.
- **Verification is annual:** Certificates expire; plan renewal in advance.
- **Context matters:** Evaluate B-BBEE against company size and sector.
- **It's a journey:** Suppliers improve over time with intentional investment.
- **Buyers should verify:** Check certificates for validity before awarding contracts.
